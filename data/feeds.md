## Data science & statistics

* **Data Colada** · http://datacolada.org/feed/ · Statistics, methodology, reproducibility, and unusually good forensic analyses of research data.
* **Statistical Modeling, Causal Inference, and Social Science** · http://andrewgelman.com/feed/ · Andrew Gelman and collaborators on Bayesian statistics, causal inference, research methodology, and how statistical practice goes wrong.
* **Statistical Thinking** · https://www.fharrell.com/index.xml · Frank Harrell brings a strong, opinionated perspective to modeling, prediction, Bayesian methods, p-values, and sound statistical practice.
* **Probably Overthinking It** · https://allendowney.blogspot.com/feeds/posts/default · Allen Downey uses computation and first-principles reasoning to make probability, Bayesian statistics, and surprising statistical problems approachable.
* **Julia Silge** · https://juliasilge.com/blog/index.xml · Practical, approachable data science with strong end-to-end examples of exploration, visualization, modeling, evaluation, and R tooling.
* **DatasFrame — Tom Augspurger** · http://tomaugspurger.github.io/feeds/all.atom.xml · A longtime pandas contributor writing from inside the Python data ecosystem about APIs, performance, distributed computing, and scientific software.
* **Pythonic Perambulations — Jake VanderPlas** · https://jakevdp.github.io/feeds/all.atom.xml · High-quality, if occasional, writing at the intersection of Python, NumPy, pandas, visualization, statistics, and scientific computing.

## AI research & engineering

* **Andrej Karpathy** · http://karpathy.github.io/feed.xml · Infrequent but unusually influential explanations and experiments that strip modern neural networks and LLMs down to their essential ideas.
* **Chip Huyen** · https://huyenchip.com/feed.xml · One of the best bridges between ML research and the infrastructure, evaluation, and systems work required to make AI useful in production.
* **Eugene Yan** · https://eugeneyan.com/rss/ · Applied ML from the perspective of someone building real systems, with especially strong work on recommendations, evaluation, experimentation, and LLM applications.
* **Lil’Log — Lilian Weng** · https://lilianweng.github.io/lil-log/feed.xml · Low-frequency, high-signal technical explainers that often become reference pieces on important AI research and engineering topics.
* **Sebastian Raschka** · http://sebastianraschka.com/rss_feed.xml · Prolific, implementation-oriented explanations of modern ML and LLM architectures, papers, training techniques, and model releases.
* **Pete Warden** · https://petewarden.com/feed/ · A distinctive perspective on edge AI, tiny models, local inference, embedded systems, and making useful machine learning run on constrained devices.
* **Matthew Rocklin** · http://matthewrocklin.com/blog/atom.xml · A scientific-computing and infrastructure builder exploring AI-assisted engineering, computational tools, Python, and how software development itself is changing.

## Data systems & engineering

* **Simon Willison’s Weblog** · http://feeds.simonwillison.net/swn-everything · Exceptionally high-signal experimentation across AI, databases, Python, open source, and data analysis, usually grounded in things he has actually built or tested.
* **Erik Bernhardsson** · http://erikbern.com/feed/ · Connects data infrastructure and software architecture with incentives, economics, organizations, and the realities of building technical products.
* **Haki Benita** · https://hakibenita.com/feeds/all.atom.xml · Deep, practical investigations of SQL, PostgreSQL, Python, performance, and data-heavy applications, usually starting from a real engineering problem.
* **Martin Fowler** · http://martinfowler.com/bliki/bliki.atom · Broader than data science, but consistently strong on software architecture, data engineering, AI systems, and how emerging technologies change engineering practice.
* **Julia Evans** · http://jvns.ca/atom.xml · One of the best explainers of the machinery underneath data work, including Linux, networking, Git, databases, debugging, terminals, and developer tools.
* **Anton Zhiyanov** · https://antonz.org/index.xml · Clever, compact technical work around SQL, SQLite, Python, databases, and developer tooling, with a knack for finding useful ideas off the beaten path.

## Data visualization

* **FlowingData — Nathan Yau** · http://flowingdata.com/feed/ · A consistently useful mix of visualization technique, statistical thinking, data exploration, and examples built from real public data.
* **EagerEyes — Robert Kosara** · https://eagereyes.org/feed · A research-informed, critical perspective on how visualization works, how charts communicate, and where common design assumptions break down.
